# Pre-registration (v1.3): the error model with the spellings of Sections 26.2–26.8, 29 and 30

Written and committed on 21 October 2026, before `code/extra_spellings.py` was run in scoring
mode. Nothing in this note was chosen after seeing a score.

## Why

Onur Teker (21 October 2026; v1.7 supplement, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23524253, section 4) points
out that the 23 triads of divine names in Section 28 stand, in the same order, in three more
places: the book paragraphs of Sections 26.2–26.8, the litany of Section 29 and the
observances of Section 30 (the last two stop after book 20, Fire). They give more attested
spellings of the 69 names. The error model of v1.0 admits a spelling only if it is one inner
letter from the Section 28 spelling and occurs at least twice on the Latin pages
(`code/name_variants.py`). This check asks what happens when every spelling that stands at a
name's place in those sections is admitted as well.

This is a sensitivity check alongside the original error model, not a replacement.
`code/repair.py`, `data/name_variants.json` and `results/repair.json` are not changed.

## Extraction (fixed in `code/extra_spellings.py`, docstring)

- Latin pages of Kupin's edition only; the table of contents and Kupin's square-bracketed
  editorial matter are left out; a Bodley reading in braces after a name counts as a second
  spelling at the same place.
- **Section 29**: the name(s) after "nomen tuum" / "nomina tua" in each invocation. The book is
  the place in the 23-letter alphabet of the superlative's first letter (the litany is
  alphabetical, one letter per book). Three names give names 1–3; two names give names 2–3;
  one name goes to the nearest of the book's three names.
- **Section 30**: the first word of each paragraph, and a capitalised word followed by an
  observance verb. The text runs book by book; each word is matched against the names of the
  current book and the next three.
- **Sections 26.2–26.8**: book segments start at the first anchor that names the book's
  number. The triad under a book heading gives names 1–3 by position. Any capitalised word
  after "hoc nomen", "potest dici", "dici potest", "nuncupari", "nuncupetur", "non discrepo"
  or "vocari" goes to the nearest of the segment's book's three names.
- **Nearest**: edit distance on the folded spelling to the Section 28 spelling(s); accepted
  only if at most half the spelling's length, strictly nearer than the book's other names, no
  other book's name strictly nearer, and the word is not exactly another book's Section 28
  spelling (a cross-reference). Everything else is listed as unassigned in
  `data/extra_spellings.json`, not guessed.

The extraction rules were adjusted three times while reading the extracted lists (to drop
cross-references to other books' names and to allow for Libra's absence from Section 30),
before any scoring. The list of new spellings they give is in `data/extra_spellings.json`,
committed with this note.

## Variant sets

1. **Primary**: every extracted spelling of a name that differs from its Section 28
   spelling(s) and from the spellings `name_variants.py` already admits, attested once or more.
2. **Secondary**: the same, attested at least twice in the places read.
3. **Primary plus listed**: the primary set plus three words the rules leave unassigned,
   placed as Teker and the book's order read them: Iaech and Lanay (book 23, names 1 and 3)
   and Ysiis (Venus, name 2).

Each extra spelling is admitted for its own paragraph only, as one "spelling" step (cost 1),
exactly like the spellings of v1.0. Everything else is `repair.py` unchanged: the same numeral
and counting slips, at most two steps, the same starting points, seed 20261019 and the same
100,000 random words per paragraph (drawn in the same order, so the chances are paired with
v1.0's).

## What is reported, and how it is judged

For each set, in `results/extra_spellings.json`:

1. **The four repairs.** Least cost for ISIAPO, ARICAA, REUELA and UISEUA (it cannot rise
   above one slip, since the model only grows; this is a check of the code), the number of
   derivations, and the one-slip chance for a random word, beside v1.0's figures
   (0.00044, 0.00005, 0.00002, 0.00004).
2. **Wrong code words.** How many of the 22 × 23 cross-pairings reach another paragraph's code
   word at 0, 1 and 2 slips (v1.0: none at any cost).
3. **Largest one-slip chance** over all 23 paragraphs (v1.0: 0.0027).

Judged as follows, and reported whatever the outcome:
- The v1.0 claim "no paragraph produces any of the other 22 code words, even with two slips"
  is said to **hold under the enlarged spellings** only if the count in 2 is zero at two slips
  for the primary set; otherwise the README and paper say at which cost and for which pairs it
  fails.
- The four repairs are said to **stay well beyond chance** if each one-slip chance in the
  primary set stays at or below 0.001; otherwise the new figures are given and the wording
  "three orders of magnitude" is qualified.
- v1.0's figures remain the study's main figures; these are reported beside them.

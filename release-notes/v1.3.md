# v1.3 — Book of Soyga: working paper; the repairs checked against every spelling the book gives the names

Working paper (not peer reviewed), with its code, data and results. The paper is `paper/soyga-seed-words.pdf` in this archive. v1.3 takes up comments from Onur Teker on v1.2 (21 October 2026): the edition's spellings of *Messias*, the other places the 23 name triads stand (Sections 26.2–26.8, 29 and 30), the letter each book "shows", and a caveat on the vowel test. The v1.0 to v1.2.1 results are unchanged; v1.3 adds a pre-registered sensitivity check and two small counts beside them.

**Abstract.** The thirty-six letter tables of the *Book of Soyga* (*Aldaraia*; British Library, Sloane MS 8; Bodleian Library, MS Bodley 908) were reconstructed by Jim Reeds (1998, 2006). Louv (2026) found Reeds's step function in the book's own Section 18 verse, and Teker (2026) showed that 19 of the 23 code words spelled out in Section 28 follow exactly from position numbers counted in each book's three divine names, leaving four unexplained: ISIAPO, ARICAA, REUELA and UISEUA. The code words themselves have been known since Reeds (1998); what was open is how these four are derived. This study reproduces those results from an independent transcription of Kupin's edition and tests one error model, declared before scoring, that allows a single Roman-numeral slip, a spelling the book uses elsewhere, or a slip in counting the letters. Under that model all four open code words are produced with one slip each; the chance that one slip turns the same names into a random six-letter word of the right letters is 0.00044, 0.00005, 0.00002 and 0.00004 respectively, and no paragraph produces any of the other 22 code words even with two slips. The same two kinds of slip are seen, far more often than chance, where the printed copies of the numbers disagree with each other. The ISIAPO repair (the Bodley numbers counted on *Mesias*, "Messias" with one *s*) appears to be new, and the two copies of the Cancer numbers support it: where the Sloane copy differs from the Bodley one, four of five numbers are exactly one higher, as a recount on the two-*s* spelling would make them (chance about 0.0001); the edition never spells the name with one *s*, so this is a counting slip, not a spelling. Version 1.3 adds, following comments from Teker, a pre-registered check that admits every spelling of the 69 names found where the same triads stand in Sections 26.2–26.8, 29 and 30: the four repairs, their chance figures and the absence of wrong code words are unchanged. No compact rule generates the positions; the words are vowel-rich and draw on all three names, and the evidence weighs toward free choice by the author, though a rule that favours pronounceable words is not ruled out.

**Results in brief**
- Reeds's rule with Louv's f = V − 1 (mod 23) reproduced: f fits all 23 letters at offset 1 only (100,000 shuffled value tables never fit more than 11); our 36 tables equal two independent implementations in all 46,656 cells.
- Code words from the three divine names: 131 of 138 letters and 19 of 23 words with every printed copy and spelling, as Teker found; 100,000 random pairings of names and paragraphs reach at most 69 letters.
- The four open words, one slip each: ISIAPO (counting slip, chance 0.00044), ARICAA (the usual spelling *Agatay*, 0.00005), REUELA (the usual spelling *Grisdecon*, 0.00002), UISEUA (*xvi* read as *vi*, 0.00004). The *Agatay* and *Grisdecon* spellings were noticed by Teker and *xvi* → *vi* by Louv; the ISIAPO repair and the single declared model covering all four are new here.
- Cancer (v1.2): the Bodley numbers 4, 15, 4, 5, 7, 11 give ISIAPO on *Mesias*; the Sloane main text (Teker's reading 4, 16, 2, 6, 8, 12) differs at five places, and at four of them is exactly one higher and gives the right letter on *Messias*. Random changes would do that about 1 time in 10,000.
- New in v1.3, *Mesias* is a counting slip, not a spelling: on the Latin pages of the edition *Messias* stands 10 times and *Messyas* 7, and no one-*s* form occurs anywhere in it (Teker's count, confirmed).
- New in v1.3, a pre-registered sensitivity check: declared rules pick out 276 occurrences of the names in Sections 26.2–26.8, 29 and 30, giving 26 spellings of 20 names that the v1.0 model did not admit (8 attested twice or more). With them admitted, the four repairs keep one slip each and the same chance figures, the largest one-slip chance over all 23 paragraphs stays 0.0027, and 0 of 22 × 23 cross-pairings reach another paragraph's code word at 0, 1 or 2 slips, in all three declared sets. 64 words the rules could not place are listed, not guessed.
- New in v1.3, Teker's observation reproduced: in the book paragraphs of Sections 26.2–26.8 the second letter each book shows is its place in the 23-letter alphabet in 13 of 16 clear cases (misses: B for k, m for n, G for s).
- The error model admits no wrong answer: 0 of 22 × 23 cross-pairings, even with two slips.
- The printed copies disagree by the same kinds of slip: one-numeral slips 28 % against 8 % for random lists; counting slips 17 % against 0.2 %.
- No rule for the positions: vowels 82 of 138 against 61.5 expected (p ≈ 0.0004); 20 of 23 words draw on all three names (p ≈ 0.02); five compact rule families fail.

**Limits.** Everything is read from Kupin's edition; as far as Teker knows, neither manuscript is digitised. The Cancer numbers are marked uncertain in the edition. The error model was written after reading the earlier work, so it was not blind to the three repairs already noticed by Teker and Louv. As Teker points out, a word chosen to be pronounceable is vowel-rich however it was found, so the vowel test separates "chosen" from "random positions" much better than from "generated by a rule that also favours pronounceable words". The v1.3 extraction rules were fixed before scoring but adjusted while reading the extracted lists.
